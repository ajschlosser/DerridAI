# 0.62.6 — Luminous Lynx

The Corpus Builder and the components it shares with the rest of the app now take their colours, type sizes and status styles from design tokens. The main effect is that dark mode works there: it was already a setting, but most of the Corpus Builder ignored it, so text disappeared into the background and buttons stayed white.

- **Dark mode.** Across all 132 Corpus Builder stories, 51 had unreadable text in dark mode before; none do now. Surfaces, form controls, buttons and status colours follow the theme, and native controls and scrollbars are dark too.
- **Status colours.** Info, ok, warning and danger each have a text, tint and outline colour, chosen so text is at least 4.5:1 and outlines at least 3:1, in both schemes. Accent-coloured text was unreadable on dark surfaces because it used the fill colour; it now has its own colour that follows whichever accent theme is chosen.
- **Increased contrast and forced colors.** Status outlines take the text colour under increased contrast, and forced-colors mode keeps every card and button outlined so structure never depends on colour.
- **Type.** Sizes in the changed styles are in `rem`, so they follow the reader's font-size setting (12px stays the floor). A secondary text colour fills the gap between primary and muted text.
- **Layout and structure fixes found on the way.** The "Run metadata enrichment again" button no longer overflows its card. The printed-page mapping table put inputs directly inside table rows, which assistive technology rejects; they are now in cells. A dialog whose body scrolls can now be scrolled from the keyboard, and only takes a tab stop while it actually overflows. A faded "unavailable" state used `opacity`, which dropped its text below AA; it is now a dashed outline.
- **Tokens and guardrails.** The tokens are in `web/src/styles/tokens.css` and described in `docs/DESIGN_TOKENS.md`. Tests check every tone and accent pair against WCAG, another checks that no new literal colours are added to component styles, and an end-to-end sweep runs axe over every Corpus Builder story in light and dark. Existing contrast tests that read colours out of the stylesheet now resolve tokens. `scripts/migrate-css-tokens.py` is the tool used to do the migration, kept for moving other views onto tokens; it is a dry run unless given `--apply`.
- **Guidance.** AGENTS.md and CONTRIBUTING.md now ask for WCAG 2.2 AA in light and dark instead of 2.0.

## What is not done

- About 219 literal colours remain in the Languages, Record and Response FAQ views, and a few dozen solid fills (status dots, solid badges) remain in components and the global stylesheet on purpose. Those views have no stories, so I could not check them in dark mode; expect rough edges there.
- Across the whole Storybook, 9 stories still fail axe in dark mode and 4 in light, all outside the Corpus Builder (down from 112 and 30). They are listed in the pull request.
- Axe cannot judge text over a gradient, which is how one dark-mode problem got past it; I found it by looking at the screen. Look at a changed screen in dark mode as well as running the tests.
- The app shell (sidebar, header) was only touched through the shared stylesheet, not reviewed in dark mode.

## Validation

Linux, Python 3.12, Node 22, on the branch after merging `master` with the Records workspace (0.62.5):

- pytest: 208 passed. `ruff` and `mypy` passed. Vitest: 206 passed, including the new token and usage tests. `eslint`, `vue-tsc`, the production build, and the Storybook build passed.
- Playwright with axe (WCAG 2.0 to 2.2, A and AA): 120 passed and 2 skipped by design, including the new sweep of every Corpus Builder story in light and in dark.
- Across the 259 stories that existed when I measured (before the Records workspace added 4, which are clean in both schemes), axe failures went from 112 stories (561 elements) to 9 (16) in dark and from 30 (87) to 4 (4) in light, with no story failing that passed before.
- Light mode was compared pixel by pixel against `master` for all 259 stories: none changed size, and the changes I looked at were shade shifts, the button that no longer overflows, and text that differs between runs (a timestamp and a port number). Two regressions this comparison caught (near-black text turned green, and a warning tint lost) are fixed.
- Not run: a check against the real app pages, which need a signed-in session and a backend. Stories were the unit of verification.
